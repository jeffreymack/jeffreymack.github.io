SaaS Mag · Deloitte · BetterCloud · Constellation Research  ·  May 2026

The "SaaSpocalypse" Was Wrong. What's Actually Happening to Software Pricing Is More Interesting.

The narrative that AI would kill SaaS by replacing software subscriptions with AI that does the work directly was wrong about the mechanism — but right about the disruption. What's actually happening is more nuanced: SaaS pricing is bifurcating into outcome-based and seat-based models simultaneously, with agents replacing the repetitive task layer while humans move up the value chain to judgment and oversight roles.

The evidence: Salesforce's AELA (flat fee for agents), ServiceNow's restructure toward AI-native tiers, Deloitte's projection of a $45B agentic AI market by 2030 from $8.5B today. But per-seat SaaS isn't dying — it's evolving. 80% of enterprises are still deploying GenAI-enabled apps on top of their existing SaaS stacks, not replacing them. The disruption is additive in the short term and substitutive in the medium term.

The CFO conversation that's happening in every large enterprise right now: "Our SaaS spend is increasing because AI agents require software access. But headcount isn't growing at the same rate. At some point those lines cross." When they do, the per-seat SaaS model faces its first structural challenge since Salesforce invented it. The vendors that build outcome-based pricing before their customers demand it will own the next decade.

"Agents don't buy seats. They consume outcomes. The vendor that figures out how to price outcomes first will own the next enterprise software cycle."
