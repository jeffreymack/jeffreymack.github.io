Editor's Note
"The AI divide isn't between companies that have AI and those that don't. It's between companies that redesigned their operations around it — and those that just bolted it on."

Today's edition is dominated by a single, uncomfortable truth: enterprise AI investment is accelerating while ROI satisfaction is declining. The enterprises pulling ahead aren't deploying more tools — they're fundamentally rethinking how they operate.

Four signal threads run through today's stories: (1) OpenAI and Anthropic are both pivoting into consulting and deployment, signaling that model capability alone won't win the enterprise; (2) governance and control layers are becoming the new competitive moat for enterprise software; (3) the GTM stack is being rebuilt from scratch around AI orchestration; and (4) the "transformation paradox" — organizations know they need to change but culture is the actual bottleneck.

All of this is directly actionable for where you sit: consulting on AI-led GTM transformation, positioning Reejig's work-redesign narrative, and building your own voice at this intersection. Let's get into it.

Enterprise AI Has Entered Its Disappointment Trough — And That's Actually the Inflection Point

Why this matters to you

Three independent research reports released this month point to the same pattern: enterprise AI investment is accelerating while satisfaction is deteriorating. Deloitte's State of AI in the Enterprise 2026 shows only 29% of enterprises report significant ROI from generative AI, and only 23% from AI agents. Writer's enterprise AI adoption report finds 48% of organizations now call AI adoption "a massive disappointment" — up from 34% last year. Yet investment is continuing at $650 billion annually and adoption is projected to reach 40% of enterprises by year-end.

This is not a contradiction. It's a pattern as old as technology adoption: the Gartner Hype Cycle's trough of disillusionment precedes the plateau of productivity. What's different about this cycle is the speed and the stakes. The companies that navigate the trough successfully aren't waiting for better models — they're doing the organizational and operational redesign work that converts AI capability into business value. The "frontier firms" pulling ahead are identifiable: they're the ones that have redesigned job architectures, built AI-native workflows, and created measurement systems for human-AI collaboration.

This convergence of signals is your market thesis made visible. Enterprise buyers are experiencing a specific pain — significant AI investment, underwhelming results, and mounting pressure from leadership to show transformation. That gap between investment and outcome is the exact problem your advisory and consulting work solves. The timing to be loud about this couldn't be better.

Patterns Emerging

Three Signals You Should Be Tracking

01
The Implementation Gap Is Now the Central Enterprise AI Story

Every major report this week — IBM, Microsoft, Deloitte, Writer, AICC — converges on the same finding: the model capability gap is closed. The deployment and transformation gap is wide open. This shifts the market conversation from "which AI?" to "how do we actually change?" That's a services and advisory conversation, not a platform conversation.

02
Governance Is Becoming a Category, Not a Feature

ServiceNow's AI Control Tower, Microsoft's Agent 365, IBM's Sovereign Core, and Cognizant's Secure AI Services all launched within the same two-week window. The market is simultaneously deploying agents at scale and realizing that ungoverned agents are a liability. Watch for this category to consolidate aggressively over the next 18 months — and for governance to become a C-suite-level budget item.

03
The GTM Stack Is Being Rebuilt Around Orchestration, Not Activation

The era of tools that activate marketing programs (email platforms, ad managers, CMS) is giving way to orchestration infrastructure — systems that observe context, infer intent, and automatically execute multi-step workflows across channels. The teams winning in 2026 aren't running better campaigns. They're running better pipelines that generate campaigns automatically from data signals. This is the GTM architecture conversation enterprises need help with most.
