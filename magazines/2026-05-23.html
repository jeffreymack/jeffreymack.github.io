May 1, 2026 — Generally Available
Why It Matters To You

The Enterprise AI Failure Rate Is a GTM Positioning Goldmine

Patterns Emerging

Three signals hiding in the noise this week

I
The Consulting Layer Is the New Battleground
OpenAI, EY+Microsoft, and SAP's €100M partner fund are all racing to own the implementation and transformation layer between AI capability and enterprise value. This is not about models anymore — it's about who sits in the CIO's office and tells them what to build next. Boutique AI advisory firms have a narrow window to establish deep vertical credibility before the incumbents lock in the relationships.
II
Change Management Is the Differentiator No One Is Funding
The HCLTech data (43% failure rate, change management as the root cause) surfaces a pattern that's consistent across every major AI implementation study in 2026: the tech is working, the humans aren't. Yet every major vendor announcement this week led with capability and infrastructure — not adoption, not workflow redesign, not organizational readiness. The gap between what vendors sell and what enterprises actually need is widening. That gap is a market.
III
AI Distribution Has Moved Inside the Tools You Already Use
Claude for Small Business (HubSpot, QuickBooks, Canva), Google Gemini (inside every ad product), SAP Joule (inside ERP), and Microsoft Copilot (inside M365) all share the same architecture: AI embedded at the point of work, not accessed as a separate product. The era of "go to the AI tool" is ending. The era of "AI is already in your workflow" has begun. For marketers and GTM teams, this means AI literacy is now an operational requirement, not a skill you can outsource.
